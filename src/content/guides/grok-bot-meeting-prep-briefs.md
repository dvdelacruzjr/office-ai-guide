## Why this matters

Good meeting prep is often skipped because it is tedious: find the last thread, recall what was promised, check recent news about the company. Grok Bot can do the gathering for you, and it can be set up as a routine so the brief is waiting before the call.

## What you’ll need

- Grok Bot with the calendar and email access you are comfortable granting (set up through your own account settings)
- A consistent place for client notes
- A rule that briefs are for you only; nothing is sent to anyone automatically

## Step-by-step

### 1. Decide what a useful brief contains

Keep it to one screen: who is attending, the purpose, what happened last time, open items, and two questions to ask.

### 2. Ask for a one-off brief first

Test the format on a single upcoming meeting before automating anything.

```
Prepare a pre-meeting brief for my next call with [CLIENT OR PROSPECT NAME].

Use:
- The calendar invite and any agenda
- My last 3 email threads with this contact, if available
- Public information about [COMPANY NAME] from the past few months (cite sources)

Format:
1. Purpose of the meeting (one sentence)
2. Attendees and their roles
3. Where we left off, including promises either side made
4. Open items and risks
5. Two questions I should ask
6. One thing to avoid saying

Keep it under 250 words. If you cannot find something, say so rather than guessing.
```

### 3. Review and refine

Tell Grok Bot what was useful and what was noise (“skip the news section unless it affects budget”). Save the final wording.

### 4. Turn it into a routine

Ask Grok Bot to prepare the same brief automatically before client meetings, delivered to you. Check that the routine only reads and summarizes and does not send anything on your behalf.

> **Tip:** Add a line at the end of each meeting: “Update the client note with what we agreed.” Tomorrow’s brief gets better.

> **Watch out:** Verify any claim about a person or company against its source link before you mention it on a call.

## Tips

- Keep the brief short enough to read in the two minutes before the call.
- For internal meetings, swap “public information” for “last meeting’s action items.”
- Ask for a post-meeting follow-up draft, but review it before sending.

## Common pitfalls

- Granting broader inbox access than the task needs
- Treating unsourced company news as fact
- Automating before testing the format by hand

## Team Safety & Privacy Note

- Use business accounts with admin controls when connecting calendar or email.
- Redact payment data, health information, and authentication codes from anything shared with the tool.
- Briefs are drafts for you; nobody should receive them without your review.
