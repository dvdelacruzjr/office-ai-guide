## Why this matters

“Which tool should I use?” comes up constantly. The honest answer depends on the task, your plan, and your data rules, and the products change often. This guide gives you a repeatable way to decide rather than a fixed ranking.

## What you’ll need

- The task, described in one sentence
- Your company’s data and tool rules
- Access to the tools your team has approved

## Step-by-step

### 1. Classify the task

Ask what kind of work it is:

- **Read and analyze:** long documents, contracts, reports
- **Draft and rewrite:** emails, proposals, posts, policies
- **Recurring or connected work:** checking an inbox or calendar, producing a regular digest
- **Calculation or data shaping:** formulas, cleanup, structured tables

### 2. Check the data

If the task involves sensitive information, the question is not which tool is smartest but which tool and plan your company has approved. If none is approved, do not do it with AI yet.

### 3. Match the task to a starting point

These are starting points based on how our guides use each tool, not rankings. Features and plans change, so check each product’s current capabilities.

| Task type | Where to start |
| --- | --- |
| Close reading of long documents, SOPs, tone-sensitive writing | Claude |
| Recurring, agent-style workflows like digests and calendar or inbox routines | Grok Bot |
| Structured drafts, meeting action plans, spreadsheet help | ChatGPT |
| Anything where your team already has an approved tool | Use that one |

### 4. Run a quick head-to-head if it matters

For a task you will repeat, give the same prompt (with non-sensitive sample content) to two tools and compare the results against a checklist: accuracy, tone, format, and effort to fix.

### 5. Decide who reviews it

Choose the human check up front: who verifies facts, who approves external messages, and what is never sent without review.

> **Tip:** Write down the winner for each recurring task in your team wiki so people stop re-deciding.

> **Watch out:** A great output from a tool you are not approved to use is still a problem.

## Tips

- Re-test every few months; products evolve.
- Do not choose by brand loyalty; choose by results on your own tasks.
- Sometimes a template or a checklist is a better solution than AI.

## Common pitfalls

- Picking a tool before defining the task
- Judging tools on one prompt
- Ignoring data rules in the rush to try something

## Team Safety & Privacy Note

- Use only tools and plans your organization approves for the data involved.
- Use sample or anonymized content for head-to-head tests.
- Keep credentials out of every prompt.
