## Why this matters

Customer-facing email takes time and tone is hard to keep consistent across a team. ChatGPT can draft a first reply quickly when you give it your policies and voice. A person still owns the final message.

## What you’ll need

- A short “how we talk to customers” note (three to five bullets)
- Your real policies for refunds, delivery, scheduling, or whatever comes up most
- A list of situations that must go to a human (legal threats, safety issues, billing disputes)

## Step-by-step

### 1. Write your reply guidelines once

Capture tone (“friendly, plain, no jargon”), sign-off, and what you never promise. Save this block somewhere your team can copy it, or add it to your ChatGPT custom instructions if your plan supports them.

### 2. Use a standard reply prompt

```
You draft customer email replies for [COMPANY NAME], a [TYPE OF BUSINESS].

Our tone: [TONE NOTES]
Our policies (use only these; do not invent others):
[PASTE POLICIES]

Customer email (remove names, account numbers, and payment details first):
[PASTE EMAIL]

Write:
1. A reply under 150 words that answers the question directly
2. One sentence acknowledging their situation, without over-apologizing
3. A clear next step
4. A flag "ESCALATE" with a reason if this should go to a manager
```

### 3. Check facts before sending

Confirm dates, amounts, and promises against your systems. If the draft says something your policy does not, edit it.

### 4. Save what works

When a draft is great, save it as a template for that situation: late delivery, rescheduling, pricing question, complaint.

> **Tip:** Ask for a short and a warm version when handling an upset customer, then choose.

> **Watch out:** Do not paste payment card numbers, passwords, or health details into the chat, and do not let the draft promise refunds or timelines you cannot confirm.

## Tips

- Keep one prompt per department so policies stay accurate.
- Ask ChatGPT to rewrite a harsh internal reply into customer-friendly language.
- Review a sample of sent replies monthly to keep quality high.

## Common pitfalls

- Sending drafts unedited
- Pasting full customer records instead of the minimum needed
- Letting different staff use different tones with no shared guideline

## Team Safety & Privacy Note

- Replace names and identifiers with placeholders when possible.
- Use the plan and data settings your company has approved for customer information.
- Escalate complaints involving safety, legal claims, or sensitive personal data to a person.
