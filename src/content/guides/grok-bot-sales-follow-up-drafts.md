## Why this matters

Most lost deals are not rejections; they are silence. A follow-up routine catches the quiet ones. Grok Bot is a good fit because follow-up is recurring and rule-based, but the output should always be drafts that you review.

## What you’ll need

- Grok Bot connected to the email and calendar accounts you choose to share
- Your follow-up rules (for example: no reply to a quote after five business days)
- 2–3 past emails you were happy with, as voice examples
- A firm rule: **drafts only, no auto-sending**

## Step-by-step

### 1. Define “needs a follow-up”

Write the rules in plain English: which threads count, how long to wait, and who to skip (existing clients, people who asked for space).

### 2. Teach it your voice

Paste two or three of your own past follow-ups (with client names removed) and ask Grok Bot to describe your style in a few bullets. Correct the description.

### 3. Run it manually first

```
Look through my sent email from the last 14 days.
Find threads where I sent a quote, proposal, or meeting recap and have not received a reply in [NUMBER] business days.

For each one, give me:
- Contact name and company
- Date and subject of my last message
- A short follow-up draft in my voice (under 90 words) that adds one useful thing, not just "checking in"
- A suggested next step if there is still no reply

Draft only. Do not send anything. Skip anyone tagged as an existing client or who asked me to pause.
```

### 4. Schedule a weekly routine

Once the drafts are consistently good, ask Grok Bot to run the same review weekly and deliver the list to you. Keep the routine limited to reading and drafting.

### 5. Review, edit, send

Open each draft, check the facts (prices, dates, names), and send it yourself.

> **Tip:** Ask for two variants per draft: one short, one with a clear question. Pick the one that fits the relationship.

> **Watch out:** A routine that sends email automatically can embarrass you fast. Keep it to drafts.

## Tips

- Add a “last touch” date column to your CRM or sheet so you can see patterns.
- Vary the angle in follow-ups: a relevant resource, a clarifying question, a deadline.
- Stop after a set number of attempts and mark the deal as cold.

## Common pitfalls

- Generic “just checking in” messages
- Following up with someone who already replied on another thread
- Letting automation decide who to contact

## Team Safety & Privacy Note

- Remove payment details and personal data from any text you paste.
- Use a business account with admin controls.
- Follow your local rules on commercial email, and honor unsubscribe requests.
